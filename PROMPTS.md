# AI / LLM Usage

## Tools used

Claude

- Front end was heavily written by Claude as I wanted to experiment with how far it could be pushed to produce an application. It only has by hand edits in one file code wise, and I did end up wordsmithing most of the visible text myself.

- The backend was largely untouched by Claude and was primarily done by hand. There is a couple of exceptions in that the Javadoc was generated by Claude as well as the Dockerfile. I also let Claude fix three minor issues it caught while creating the Javadoc and the corresponding tests on those three issues.

## Where it helped vs. where you steered it

_Where the assistant sped you up, and where you had to correct, override, or throw away
its output._

One of the biggest speed increases I saw was in the creating the compose.yaml and the Dockerfiles. I gave the prompt a one liner asking for a multistage Docker setup with the details of the stack and it pretty much gave me a working solution out of the box.

The Javadocs creation was also brilliant magic. I thought that it was going to be an exercise where I would have Claude scaffold it, and then I would heavily edit. In all honesty I had seen the copy on the frontend and the writing style of the frontend code comments so my optimism was somewhat low. It was instead incredibly good. 

Claude Design I'm also positive on. It did produce a usable shape design fairly quickly and I should have stopped there and started working on the port. I spent too much time and tokens getting it perfect it Claude Design to only watch the port not be super exact and spend time getting it back to the state I had it in Claude Design. I essentially did the same work twice.

I also enjoyed asking Claude to just give me options to consider when I reached decision points.

The initial table designs were a bit of a chore. The spacing on the columns were wildly inconsistent and directives to make the columns uniform or evenly spaced were not working out. I had to rewind a few times. The solutions were also fragile in that I would get them to look okish and adding/removing a column just made it go back to looking funny. After a few rounds of struggle it took me being incredibly explicit in the behavior I was seeing with direct column examples that were bad for it to click with Claude and it produced a design that worked well.

## Decisions you made against its suggestion

_Anything you chose to do differently from what the assistant proposed, and why._

The main category of suggestions that I went against were situations were Claude didn't understand something or was very confidently wrong. I was generally able to steer it back as with most things misunderstandings can be resolved with a conversation. It certainly works both ways as Claude raised a concern, and I misunderstood what it meant. I probed further to realize that Claude was right and I was the problem.
